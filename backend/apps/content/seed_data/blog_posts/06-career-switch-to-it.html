Many people start thinking about a career switch to IT in their late twenties or thirties. Maybe growth in your current field has slowed, maybe you want remote work options, or maybe you have simply discovered that you enjoy solving problems with technology. The good news: age is not a barrier in tech. Thousands of developers, analysts and testers started their careers after 30. This guide shows you how to do it sensibly.

Is it too late to switch to IT after 30?

No. Technology companies hire for skills, and many value the maturity, communication and domain knowledge that career changers bring. A former accountant who learns data analysis understands finance data better than most freshers. A former teacher who becomes a developer often explains technical ideas clearly. Your past experience is an advantage if you position it well.

What is true is that you have less free time and more responsibilities than a 21-year-old student. That means you need a focused plan rather than trying to learn everything.

Step 1: Choose the right role for you

"IT" is a huge field. Pick a target role based on your interests and background:

Read job descriptions for each role in your city and notice which ones excite you. Choose one primary target.

Step 2: Use your domain knowledge

Look for roles where your old industry meets technology. Banking professionals can target fintech companies, healthcare workers can target health-tech, and sales or marketing professionals can move into analytics or marketing technology. Domain knowledge helps you stand out from candidates who only know code.

Step 3: Build a realistic learning schedule

If you are working full time, aim for 10 to 15 hours per week:

  • One to two hours on weekday evenings or early mornings.
  • Longer project sessions on weekends.
  • Fixed days and times, treated like appointments you do not cancel.

At this pace, most people can become ready for entry-level roles in eight to twelve months.

Step 4: Learn with projects, not just videos

Watching tutorials feels productive but builds little skill. After learning each concept, build something small with it. By the end of your learning plan, you should have three to five projects that relate to your target role and, ideally, to your previous industry. For example, a former retail manager learning development could build an inventory management app.

Step 5: Consider structured training

Self-learning works for some people, but many career changers benefit from a structured course with mentors, deadlines and peers. A good course saves you time deciding what to learn next and gives you feedback on your code. If you choose a course, check the syllabus, the projects you will build, the mentors' experience and whether it includes interview preparation.

Step 6: Rewrite your resume for tech

  • Put a short summary at the top that states your new target role.
  • List technical skills and projects before past work experience.
  • Rewrite past job achievements to highlight transferable skills: problem-solving, process improvement, stakeholder communication, working with data.
  • Include links to GitHub, a portfolio site or deployed projects.

Step 7: Network and apply consistently

Career changers often get their first opportunity through people, not job portals. Connect with developers and recruiters on LinkedIn, attend tech meetups, and tell former colleagues you are moving into tech. Apply to internships, trainee programmes and junior roles. Also look inside your current company: internal moves into IT teams are often the easiest route.

Step 8: Prepare for interviews

Interviewers will ask why you are switching. Prepare a short, positive answer that connects your past experience to your new role. Then practise technical questions for your target role and be ready to explain every line of your projects. Use the interview preparation section and the AI mock interview to practise.

Common worries, answered

  • "I will be competing with younger people." You will also bring professionalism and experience that many freshers lack.
  • "I might have to accept a lower salary at first." This can happen, so plan your finances. Growth in tech roles can be quick once you have experience.
  • "I am not good at maths." Most development and testing roles need logic, not advanced maths.

Your first 30 days

  1. Week 1: Research roles and choose one target.
  2. Week 2: Set up your tools and learn the first fundamentals.
  3. Week 3: Build your first tiny project.
  4. Week 4: Create a GitHub profile, update LinkedIn and join one community.

A: It depends on your interests.
